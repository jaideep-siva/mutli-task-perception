Single-pass multitask network: backbone extracts hierarchical feature maps at 3 spatial scales. FPN fuses them top-down so every detection scale sees both fine-grained texture and high-level context. FCOS detection head is anchor-free — each cell predicts LTRB distances to the nearest object boundary at that scale. Segmentation head upsamples from all three FPN levels for a full-resolution lane mask. No separate models, no shared-nothing pipeline — one forward pass, two outputs.

Why is Lane IoU so high (90%+)? Segmentation is a binary task — lane vs. background — with consistent visual structure. The head fuses all 3 FPN scales so it sees both fine edge detail and broad scene context.
Why is mAP modest (~0.25)? Only ~1,400 annotated frames. Early stopping cut both runs short. Classes like pothole and unknown have high visual variance and are rare per image — more data via pseudo-labelling will lift these.
Why does ResNet-18 beat ConvNeXt overall? ConvNeXt-B trained only 13/50 epochs before early stopping — heavily penalised by limited data. Its 206 ms latency also makes it impractical regardless of accuracy improvements.
Why is ConvNeXt mAP@75 higher (0.125 vs 0.098)? Larger receptive field (7×7 depthwise kernels) produces tighter box localisation. Higher mAP@75 means fewer but better-placed detections — quality over quantity.
ResNet-18 wins on every practical metric for deployment: lowest latency, lowest memory, highest IoU, best FPS. ConvNeXt's only advantage is mAP@75 (tighter boxes), but it barely trained and is 13× slower. Per-class AP: pedestrian and barrel are reliably detected because they're larger and more numerous. Stop sign and unknown suffer from equirectangular distortion in the image periphery and class imbalance. Pseudo-labelling (Step 3) will expand training data ~4× and should lift all classes significantly.

The headline story: our ResNet-18 under TensorRT FP16 is projected at ~8ms — placing us in the same latency bracket as Mobileye and Tesla FSD inference, both systems that cost hundreds of millions to develop. At 15.9ms in vanilla PyTorch we're already at 62.9 FPS, which is 4× the 15 FPS minimum for smooth obstacle avoidance. Critically, we do detection AND segmentation in one forward pass. A competing approach using two separate networks doubles the latency budget. Our 452 MB footprint runs on a Jetson Orin NX (16 GB) with plenty of headroom for preprocessing, postprocessing, and ROS 2 messaging.
